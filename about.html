Fibre deserves its own brand.

Protein had its moment in India. Fibre hasn't yet, even though most of us don't eat enough of it. FibreFit exists to make fibre something you understand, enjoy and take every day.

Why we started

When we looked at the fibre on Indian shelves, we found two kinds. Traditional isabgol, trusted but sold like a medicine for one problem. And premium imported fibres that cost too much to take every day.

What we didn't find was a fibre brand for everyday life: one that tastes fine, fits a normal routine, and explains plainly what's inside and why.

So we built FibreFit around three rules:

  • Every gram is on the label. You see each fibre and exactly how much of it is in your sachet.
  • We say how sure the science is. Well proven, some proof, or early days. We'd rather under-promise.
  • Food first. A sachet adds to dals, millets, fruit and vegetables. It doesn't replace them.

Who fibre is for

Adults need 25 to 30 g of fibre a day. Here's where it most often goes missing, and how the right fibres fit in.

High-protein diets

Gym-goers often fill their plates with protein and leave less room for whole grains, fruit and vegetables. Fibre fills that gap.

Fits well: easy-mixing fibres like PHGG and resistant dextrin, or a protein and fibre blend.

Long desk days

Long hours sitting, quick meals and too little water make it easy to fall short on fibre without noticing.

Fits well: a daily sachet in a glass of water, with psyllium for bulk and regularity.

Feeling full between meals

Gel-forming fibres slow how fast food leaves your stomach, so you may feel full for longer. The effect on weight is small, so think of it as a helper, not a diet.

Fits well: psyllium and fenugreek, the thick, gel-forming fibres.

Women's daily nutrition

Women need about 25 g of fibre a day, and many get far less. A steady daily habit is the simplest way to close the gap.

Fits well: a gentle mix of PHGG, inulin and acacia that's easy on the stomach.

Older adults

Digestion often slows with age. Gentle, low-thickness fibres and a slow start help the body adjust without discomfort.

Fits well: PHGG and acacia fibre, started at half a dose.

Families and kids

Children who skip vegetables miss out on fibre too. Smaller amounts in a form they'll actually eat matter more than big doses.

Fits well: pectin-based gummies and mild, soluble fibres. Check with your paediatrician first.

This is general information about fibre, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor if you are pregnant, nursing, have diabetes or take regular medicines.
