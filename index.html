Why eat more fibre?

A major review in The Lancet in 2019, commissioned by the WHO, pooled 185 studies and 58 clinical trials. People who ate more fibre had lower risks of serious disease.

For every extra 8 g of fibre a day, people had

19%lower risk of coronary heart disease
15%lower risk of type 2 diabetes
7%lower risk of death from any cause

The biggest benefits were seen at 25 to 29 g of fibre a day. These are links seen across many thousands of people, not proof that fibre alone causes them, and not a promise about any single product.

What fibre does inside you

Swells and adds bulk

Some fibre soaks up water and turns into a soft gel. You feel full for longer and stay regular.

Feeds your gut bacteria

Trillions of bacteria live in your large intestine. You can't digest fibre, but they can.

Makes helpful substances

As bacteria break fibre down they make short-chain fatty acids, which the lining of your gut uses for energy.

Gym-goers often fill their plates with protein and leave less room for whole grains, fruit and vegetables. Fibre fills that gap.

Long hours sitting, quick meals and too little water make it easy to fall short on fibre without noticing.

Feeling full between meals

Gel-forming fibres slow how fast food leaves your stomach, so you may feel full for longer. The effect on weight is small, so think of fibre as a helper, not a diet.

Fits well: psyllium and fenugreek, the thick, gel-forming fibres.

Women's daily nutrition

Women need about 25 g of fibre a day, and many get far less. A steady daily habit is the simplest way to close the gap.

Fits well: a gentle mix of PHGG, inulin and acacia that's easy on the stomach.

½ sachet a day for the first week

Older adults

Digestion often slows with age. Gentle, low-thickness fibres and a slow start help the body adjust without discomfort.

Fits well: PHGG and acacia fibre, started at half a dose.

26% eat multigrains every day

Families and kids

Children who skip vegetables miss out on fibre too. Small amounts in a form they'll actually eat matter more than big doses.

Fits well: pectin-based gummies and mild, soluble fibres. Check with your paediatrician first.

How to take it

Too much fibre too fast can cause gas and bloating, so the first week is gentle.

  1. Start with half

    Days 1 to 7: stir half a sachet into a full glass of water (about 240 ml) and drink it straight away.

  2. Then one a day

    From day 8: one sachet a day, mixed the same way.

  3. Keep water close

    Drink plenty of water through the day. Take it at least 2 hours apart from any medicines.

Questions

What's in Daily Fibre?

Each 12 g sachet has 6 g psyllium husk, 2 g partially hydrolysed guar gum (PHGG), 1.5 g fenugreek seed fibre, 1.5 g millet-bran fibre and 1 g green-banana resistant starch.

Is it a laxative?

No. Daily Fibre is a fibre supplement meant to sit alongside a fibre-rich diet. It isn't intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.

Why start with half a sachet?

Adding a lot of fibre quickly can cause gas and bloating. A week at half a sachet gives your gut time to adjust.

Can I take it with my medicines?

Fibre can slow how medicines are absorbed. Take Daily Fibre at least 2 hours apart from medicines, and ask your doctor or pharmacist what's right for you.

Who shouldn't take it?

Don't use it if you have trouble swallowing, or a narrowed or blocked food pipe or bowel. Psyllium can cause allergic reactions in sensitive people.

Talk to your doctor first if you are pregnant, nursing, have diabetes or take regular medicines.
