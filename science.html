How fibre works

What fibre does inside you, how much you need, and which benefits are well proven versus still being studied. We tell you which is which.

What is fibre?

Fibre is the part of plant food your body can't digest. It's in whole grains, millets, dals, vegetables, fruit and seeds. It passes through you, and on the way it does useful work.

What happens to fibre inside you

  1. It swells and adds bulk. Some fibre soaks up water and turns soft and gel-like. This helps your stomach feel full and keeps bowel movements regular.
  2. It feeds the good bacteria in your gut. Trillions of bacteria live in your large intestine. You can't digest fibre, but they can.
  3. The bacteria make helpful substances. As they break fibre down, they make short-chain fatty acids that the lining of your gut uses for energy. Some travel in the blood to other parts of the body.

Without enough fibre, these bacteria go hungry. In one study in mice, hungry bacteria began to eat the protective lining of the gut. We don't yet know if the same happens in people.

The big picture

A large review in The Lancet in 2019, commissioned by the WHO, pooled 185 observational studies and 58 clinical trials. People who ate the most fibre, around 25 to 29 g a day, had the lowest risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and early death. Each extra 8 g a day was linked to 7% lower risk of death from any cause, 15% lower risk of type 2 diabetes and 19% lower risk of coronary heart disease.

These are links seen across many thousands of people. They don't prove that fibre alone causes the difference, and no single product can promise them.

How much fibre do you need?

The ICMR-NIN guideline for adults is 25 g a day for women and 30 g a day for men. Many Indian adults eat less than this.

Different fibres do different things

Eating a mix of fibres gives your body a bit of everything.

Gel-forming fibres

Psyllium (isabgol) and fenugreek (methi). They slow digestion and help with fullness and cholesterol.

Food for gut bacteria

Guar, green banana and millet bran. Your gut bacteria ferment them.

Bulk fibres

Millet bran and whole grains. They help keep bowel movements regular.

How to start safely

  • Start slowly. Too much fibre too fast can cause gas and bloating.
  • Drink plenty of water, and always take psyllium with a full glass of water.
  • If you take regular medicines, ask your doctor or pharmacist when to take fibre. Fibre can slow how medicines are absorbed.
  • Don't use psyllium if you have trouble swallowing or a blockage in your food pipe or bowel.
  • Talk to your doctor first if you are pregnant, nursing, have diabetes or take regular medicines.
